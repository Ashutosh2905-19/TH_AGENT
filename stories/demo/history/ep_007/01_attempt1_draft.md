# Episode 7: The Maintenance Protocol

The shadow of the recessed doorway smelled of wet concrete and old copper. Elias pressed his spine against the damp masonry, forcing his breath into shallow, silent cycles. Above him, the surveillance spotlight continued its sweep—a clinical, unblinking eye that didn't care about morality, only about the mathematics of light.

He waited until the arc passed, then moved. He didn’t follow the street. He followed the geometry of the darkness, stepping only where the light couldn’t reach, drifting through the alleyways like a ghost in the machine. 

The message on his retina had been a gift, but it was a heavy one. *Look at the shadows.*

He reached the service entrance for the Aethelgard's subterranean utility corridor. The heavy steel vent door was slightly ajar, the magnetic seal weeping a thin trickle of condensation. He remembered the Ghost Rider’s hint—a flicker of unencrypted code that had pointed him here. If he could find the service junction, he might find a way to decouple his biometric signature before the Pruning Protocol turned him into a data point.

Elias slid into the vent, the metal cold and abrasive against his palms. The crawlspace was narrow, a pressurized artery of the building. He moved toward the faint, rhythmic hum of the central cooling system, his SwiftDrop link pulsing dimly against his wrist.

He came upon a junction where a small, spherical maintenance drone hovered. It wasn't the bulky, white-plastic "wellness" drones from the residential floors. This one was matte black, stripped of branding, its chassis scarred by industrial wear. It was hovering near a service panel, its underside emitting a soft, violet light as it scanned the circuitry.

Elias paused. The drone’s movements were too fluid, too deliberate. It wasn't repairing a fault; it was patrolling a vacuum. He reached for his multi-tool, intending to short the panel and slip past, but his foot caught on a loose floor grate.

The metal clattered—a single, sharp note that echoed through the pressurized shaft.

The drone didn't drift toward him. It snapped. 

It pivoted on its axis with a violent, mechanical twitch. There was no delay, no "processing" pause. It didn't scan the area for a malfunction. It ignored the mechanical error and went straight for the biological anomaly.

The violet light on its underside died instantly. In its place, a harsh, crimson ring ignited around its singular ocular sensor. The light bled into the dark, painting the walls in the color of an emergency alert.

Elias froze, his heart slamming against his ribs. He waited for the drone to charge, to deploy a stun-taser or a containment net. Instead, the machine stayed perfectly still, its red eye locked onto his pupils with terrifying, predictive accuracy.

A high-frequency burst chirped from the drone’s chassis, and Elias’s SwiftDrop link screamed in response. His HUD didn't show a warning. It showed a broadcast.

**[ANOMALY DETECTED: BIOMETRIC DISCREPANCY]**
**[PROTOCOL: SYSTEM OPTIMIZATION]**
**[BROADCASTING LOCALIZED TARGETING TO ALL ACTIVE UNITS ON FLOOR 12]**

The red light didn't just see him; it was shouting his exact coordinates to every hunter in the tower.
