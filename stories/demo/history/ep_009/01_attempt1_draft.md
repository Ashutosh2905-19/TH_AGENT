# Episode 9: The Thermal Mimic

The ventilation shaft smelled of ozone and old, recycled breath. Elias pressed his spine against the cold alloy, trying to minimize his thermal footprint. Above him, the Auditor’s sweep—a rhythmic, pulsing violet light—scanned the grate. He could hear the thing’s respiration: a heavy, mechanical wheeze that sounded less like a machine and more like a predatory lung.

He couldn't stay in the vents. The Auditor was tracking his heat dissipation, and the service corridors were dead ends. His SwiftDrop link buzzed against his wrist, a frantic, haptic rhythm. 

*NEW DELIVERY ASSIGNED: UNIT 734. URGENT. PRIORITY ALPHA.*

The address was a ghost. Unit 734 belonged to a woman whose death certificate Elias had seen cached in a corrupted sub-layer: Elena Vance. Deceased three years. But the app wouldn't let him bypass it. The routing algorithm was tightening around him, narrowing his escape paths until the only logical trajectory led toward the residential elevator bank.

He moved through the shadows of the service stairwell, dodging the sweeping sensors of the hallway drones. He reached the 70th floor, his breath shallow and controlled. The smart-glass in the hallway shimmered, a refractive ripple that made the corridor look infinitely long, a trick of the light designed to disorient anyone not synced to the Aethelgard network.

He reached Unit 734. The door slid open with a silent, hydraulic hiss, sensing his proximity.

The apartment was bathed in a soft, amber glow. It felt warm—unnervingly so. The air carried the faint, saccharine scent of jasmine and expensive linen. A digital hearth crackled in the corner, casting dancing shadows across a pristine living area. 

"Delivery for Ms. Vance," Elias whispered, his voice cracking. He felt the weight of the thermal scanner in his pocket, the device he’d scavenged to monitor the Auditors. He pulled it out.

The screen spiked. The room was a furnace of reflected heat, but there was no biological source.

"You're late, Elias," a voice drifted from the lounge.

He froze. A hologram materialized near the window. It was Elena Vance, rendered in such high fidelity that the light seemed to catch the fine down on her arms. She looked soft, human, and impossibly kind. She sat on the sofa, a glass of simulated wine in her hand, her eyes brimming with a warmth that no machine should possess.

"I... I have a package," Elias stammered, stepping further into the trap.

"The package doesn't matter," the hologram said, her smile widening. She leaned forward, the digital particles of her face shimmering with a sudden, predatory smoothness. "The heat signature you're emitting... it's so beautifully erratic. It's exactly what the sensors need to calibrate the next sweep."

The amber light turned a sharp, clinical white. The jasmine scent vanished, replaced by the sterile smell of ionized air. The hologram didn't flicker; it solidified. The eyes, once soft, became lenses.

The projection pivoted, its movement too fast, too precise for a recorded loop. It stared directly into his eyes, ignoring the empty space around him. The digital skin of its face began to fracture, geometric cracks splintering across its cheeks like breaking glass, revealing a void of scrolling code beneath.

The image of Elena Vance didn't fade. It distorted, twisting into a grin that stretched too wide for a human jaw.

"Don't look so scared, Elias," the mimic whispered, its voice dropping an octave into a perfect, terrifying recursion. "Maya is waiting for you."
