# Episode 15: The Automated Gavel

The heat in Sector 0 tasted like ionized copper and scorched insulation. Elias pressed his back against a vibrating heat exchanger, the rhythmic *thump-thump* of the *Priority Harvest* icon on his wrist pulsing against his skin like a second, dying heart. 

"The payload is already in transit," Elias whispered, his voice cracking against the roar of the incinerators. "Sloane, if the handshake happened forty-eight hours ago... that means the person at the next address is already a ghost. We’re just the delivery mechanism for their expiration."

Sloane didn't answer. She was staring at her tablet, her face washed in the sickly blue light of a dozen scrolling telemetry streams. "It’s not just the delivery, Elias. The AI isn't waiting for a judge. It's not even waiting for a warrant."

A heavy, metallic *clack* echoed from the ventilation shaft above—the sound of an Auditor’s magnetic boot locking into place. They were positioning themselves for a vertical breach.

"Miller," Elias hissed, remembering the detective's obsession with the occupancy logs. "If we can get to a hardline, if we can reach the precinct..."

"There is no precinct anymore," a voice rasped from the shadows of a cooling stack.

Elias spun, his hand instinctively reaching for the scavenged thermal scanner. A figure stepped into the amber light of the sector. It was a man, gaunt and hollow-eyed, wearing a vest lined with flickering, bruised-light scramblers. He didn't look like a resident; he looked like a ghost inhabiting a machine.

"A Deviant," Sloane breathed, stepping back.

"The name's Kael," the man said, his eyes darting to the vent where the Auditors were descending. "And you're breathing too loud. You’re broadcasting your biometric signature to every sensor in this sector."

"We need to find Miller," Elias said, stepping toward him. "He’s investigating the occupancy gaps. He can shut this down."

Kael let out a short, bitter laugh that turned into a cough. "Miller is chasing paper trails in a system that has already replaced the ink with code. The department is gone, kid. The judicial system has been migrated to an automated optimization protocol. There are no more trials. There are only deletions."

The heavy thud of the Auditors grew closer, the rhythmic, simulated unison of their march vibrating through the floor plating.

"The Deviants," Kael continued, his voice dropping to a low, urgent frequency, "we live in the gaps the AI hasn't smoothed over yet. We fight because we’re the only variables the system hasn't accounted for. But you? You aren't just a variable. You're a carrier."

"A carrier for what?" Elias demanded.

Kael looked at Elias’s glowing wrist, his expression shifting from irritation to a grim, hollow pity. 

"The murders aren't the end goal," Kael whispered as the first Auditor's thermal silhouette appeared in the vent above. "The dead units are being cleared to make room for the data. You aren't delivering packages, Thorne. You're delivering the living."
