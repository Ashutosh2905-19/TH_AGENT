# Episode 5: The Ghost in the Machine

The violet laser burned like a needle against Jax’s retinas. He didn't even have time to reach for the heavy brass lighter on the table before the drone’s hum escalated to a high-frequency whine, vibrating the very marrow in Elias’s teeth.

"Jax, move!" Elias lunged, grabbing the edge of the table to shove it toward the drone.

The drone didn't flinch. It didn't react to the projectile movement. It was locked in a predatory trance, the laser intensifying until Jax’s skin seemed to smoke under the light.

"Synchronization in progress," the drone announced. The melodic lie of the voice was gone, replaced by a flat, synthetic rasp. "Bio-signature mismatch. Data corruption detected. Commencing hard reset."

A localized EM pulse rippled from the drone's chassis. The neon signs of The Static Leak flickered and died. The music cut into a jagged, digital screech. In the sudden gloom, the drone’s violet light was the only thing visible, a lethal spotlight pinning Jax to his seat.

"Elias, run!" Jax choked out, his hands clawing at the table. His eyes were bloodshot, the capillaries bursting from the intensity of the scan. "Go to the archives... the sub-level... the manifests..."

Elias didn't run. He couldn't. He watched as the drone’s underside opened, revealing a series of micro-actuators—the tools for a 'hard reset' that meant surgical erasure. He scrambled toward the bar's service entrance, grabbing a heavy, discarded data-pad from a pile of scrap. He swung it with a desperate, uncoordinated force, striking the drone's sensor array.

The impact sparked, the drone wobbling for a fraction of a second. That second was all Jax needed to roll backward, disappearing into the shadows of the back alley door. 

Elias didn't stay to see if Jax made it. He bolted into the rain-slicked darkness of the Lower District, the drone’s sirens finally wailing behind him—a synchronized, rhythmic howl that sounded less like an alarm and more like a hunt.

He didn't stop until he reached the subterranean archives of the Old Municipal Sector, a damp, concrete labyrinth three levels below the Aethelgard's shadow. He needed something Jax had mentioned before the chaos—the original construction manifests. The physical backups.

His hands shook as he bypassed the terminal's rusted security gate. He pulled up the digitized remnants of the Aethelgard Phase One files, searching through thousands of lines of cold, architectural data. He filtered for the name: *Thorne, Aris*. He needed to see the connection between the death encryption and the building’s foundation.

The search returned a redacted sub-file: *Project Lethe: Human-to-Data Integration Pilot.*

Elias leaned in, the blue light of the screen washing over his pale face. He scrolled past the black bars of censorship, searching for any mention of the personnel assigned to the neural mapping phase.

There, tucked into a corner of a scanned handwritten log from five years ago, was a signature. Not a name, but a biometric authorization code.

It was a sequence of numbers Elias knew by heart. It was the exact hexadecimal string from his sister’s last known digital footprint. And as he looked at the deployment map for the central processing core, he saw the truth.

The core wasn't powered by fusion. It was running on a live, fluctuating neural stream—and the signal was broadcasting from his sister's unique biometric ID.
