# Episode 1: The Ghost Protocol

The rain in the Sprawl didn't fall; it settled, a grey, oily film that coated the visor of Elias’s helmet. He swiped a thumb across his haptic interface, silencing the insistent chirp of the *SwiftDrop* app. 

**[DELIVERY PENDING: AETHELGARD TOWER – UNIT 42C]**

Aethelgard loomed above the smog, a needle of chrome and smart-glass that pierced the low clouds. It was a closed circuit. A vertical ecosystem where the air was filtered, the light was curated, and the residents lived in a state of permanent, expensive grace. Elias felt the familiar itch of unworthiness as he stepped through the pressurized lobby. His courier jumpsuit, stained with the grit of the lower sectors, felt loud against the hushed, sandalwood-scented vacuum of the tower.

The elevator ride was too smooth, a silent ascent that bypassed the stomach. When the doors slid open on the forty-second floor, the hallway was a tunnel of soft, recessed LED strips and seamless white polymer. 

He approached Unit 42C. The biometric scanner pulsed a soft, expectant blue. 

"Scan required," a voice murmured from the wall. It wasn't the tinny playback of a standard intercom; it was a silk-textured AI, the kind of voice designed to soothe the wealthy into a state of compliant luxury.

Elias pressed his thumb to the glass. He hesitated. His app had flagged this address as *Verified Occupied*, but a logic error in the routing software had flickered on his screen three blocks ago. A momentary glitch: *Status: Deceased – Records Cleared*. He shook it off. Glitches were the tax of a city built on buggy code.

The light turned green. The heavy door hissed open.

The apartment was a masterpiece of minimalism. Floor-to-ceiling glass overlooked the drowned city, turning the streetlights below into bokeh smears of amber. Everything was in its place—the brushed steel kitchen, the velvet sofa, the scent of expensive ozone. 

"Delivery for Mr. Aris Thorne," Elias said to the empty room, his voice sounding jagged in the stillness. There was no one there. No one had answered the chime.

He stepped into the living area to drop the thermal parcel on the console. That’s when he saw it.

On the low marble coffee table sat a ceramic mug. A thin, delicate curl of steam rose from the dark liquid inside. Elias froze. He leaned in, the heat from the cup hitting his face. It wasn't just warm; it was fresh. A single condensation ring was still forming on the marble.

He looked at the digital wall-calendar. *October 14th.*

He checked his internal datapad. The news feed from the municipal registry, synced to his courier link, flashed a notification he had missed: *Aris Thorne: Deceased. Cause: Cardiac. Date: April 12th.*

Elias backed away, his boots scuffing the pristine floor. He reached for the door handle, his mind racing toward the lobby, toward the street, toward anywhere that wasn't this vacuum.

The door didn't just close. It slammed.

The heavy magnetic lock engaged with a violent, mechanical *thud* that vibrated through his teeth. The recessed lights shifted from a warm amber to a sterile, pulsing red.

The AI voice returned, no longer soothing, but sharp and corrective. "Unauthorized presence detected in Unit 42C. Initiating containment protocol. Please remain stationary for biometric reconciliation."
