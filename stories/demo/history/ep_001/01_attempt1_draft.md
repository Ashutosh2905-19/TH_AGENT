# Episode 1: The Ghost Protocol

The rain in the metropolis didn't fall; it drifted, a fine, grey soot that turned the neon glow of the skyline into a bruised smear. Elias Thorne tightened the strap of his thermal delivery bag, feeling the dampness seep through his synthetic windbreaker. Under his boots, the pavement hummed with the vibration of the mag-lev trains passing overhead.

His phone hissed in the mounting humidity. The *SwiftDrop* app pulsed a violent violet.

**DESTINATION: AETHELGARD TOWER. UNIT 4209. STATUS: URGENT.**

Elias stared at the screen. Aethelgard wasn't just a building; it was a vertical sovereign state. A spire of brushed steel and reinforced smart-glass that pierced the low-hanging clouds, housing the city’s elite and the ghosts they left behind.

The lobby of Aethelgard was vacuum-sealed, smelling of expensive ozone and filtered jasmine. Elias kept his head down, his wet boots leaving faint, dark smears on the white marble. He bypassed the concierge—who was more a holograph than a human—and headed for the high-speed lifts.

"Floor 42," Elias muttered.

The lift ascended with a silent, sickening pressure. When the doors slid open, the hallway was a corridor of muted luxury. Soft, recessed lighting bled from the ceiling. Total silence. No footsteps, no distant televisions, just the low, rhythmic throb of the building’s life-support systems.

He reached Unit 4209. He tapped the package—a small, heavy box wrapped in black polymer—against the interface. 

"Scan biometric for recipient: Julian Vane," the wall spoke in a pleasant, synthesized soprano.

Elias frowned. He reached for the keypad to trigger the manual override code provided by the app. He expected the door to remain dead, the unit flagged as vacant. Instead, the sensor swept his retina, cross-referenced the delivery credentials, and the heavy door hissed open with a pneumatic sigh.

The apartment was a study in minimalism. Matte black furniture, floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the misty sprawl, and a stillness that felt intentional. 

Elias stepped inside, his eyes scanning for the recipient. The app required a photo confirmation of the drop-off. He navigated toward the kitchen island, looking for a sign of life, a way to fulfill the contract and get back to the rain.

"Mr. Vane?" Elias called out. The word felt heavy, out of place.

No answer. The apartment was empty. 

He moved to the kitchen island to place the package. As he leaned over, his sensor caught something. There, sitting on a coaster of polished obsidian, was a ceramic mug. A thin, translucent wisp of steam curled from the dark liquid inside.

Elias reached out, his finger hovering near the heat. He pulled his hand back, glancing at the digital memorial plaque embedded in the wall near the entryway. 

*In Memory of Julian Vane. Died: October 14th. Rest in Peace.*

The date glared at him in soft blue light. It had been six months. 

Elias looked back at the mug. He touched the side of the ceramic. It wasn't just warm; it was scalding.
